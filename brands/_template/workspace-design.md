# {{brand_name}} — provisional design guide

Status: starter proposal. Confirm the audience, identity, colours, fonts and publication formats from this brand's own approved material before treating these rules as established. Do not borrow another company's identity. Keep unconfirmed decisions labelled in the review handoff.

## Voice and content
Use clear, direct language and sentence-case headlines. Lead with one message and a specific next action where appropriate. Match the user's requested language. Preserve approved names, claims, prices, dates and legal wording. Ask for missing facts when they block the design; do not invent them. These are provisional editorial choices, not recovered brand rules.

## Identity and transparent assets
Use supplied original marks with their proportions intact. Keep logos separate from photography, panels, type and motifs. Use transparent SVG masters or genuine transparent PNGs for placement. Never use a checkerboard image as transparency or present a raster wrapped in SVG as vector artwork. Maintain source clear-space and minimum-size rules where provided. Do not fabricate a logo when no approved mark is available.

## Colour
Use the separately configured brand palette. The first colour is the primary colour; other entries carry supporting roles. Check foreground/background contrast in the actual composition. This scaffold's palette is provisional and must be replaced or explicitly adopted for this brand. Keep gradients and extra colours out until supported by approved references or the brief.

## Typography
Use the separately configured heading font for titles and the body font for reading text, captions, labels and charts. Check font availability, permitted use and language coverage. Record any substitute. Use a clear hierarchy and consistent alignment. Reflow or edit approved copy when needed; do not solve overflow with unreadably small text.

## Layout and hierarchy
Start with the audience, message and placement. Align components to a deliberate grid. Keep generous outer margins, consistent spacing and one dominant focal point. Separate titles, supporting copy, evidence, CTA and required notices. Keep text and key identity elements clear of busy imagery. Review at the intended viewing size.

## Publication formats
The scaffold provides three proposed static layouts, not verified platform requirements:
- Portrait announcement: 1080 × 1350, 4:5. Headline, optional eyebrow, body, details and CTA.
- Square card: 1080 × 1080, 1:1. One main message with shorter supporting copy.
- Landscape graphic: 1600 × 900, 16:9. Adapt the hierarchy for a wide surface.
Replace these with the brand's documented recurring families. Treat campaigns, evergreen content, informational posts and partner material as distinct families when the evidence supports it. For carousels, define cover, content and closing cards; review every card. For vertical stories or video covers, establish destination dimensions and safe areas before producing them. Recompose each format rather than stretching a finished layout.

## Images, illustration and icons
Follow the separate Image style field and the approved reference library. Preserve real subjects and product details. Keep photography, illustration, icons and decorative shapes separate in the master. Use a consistent icon family. Do not present generated scenes as documentary evidence.

## Slides, documents, charts and web pages
Apply the same palette, font roles and spacing logic across media. Give each slide one primary message; keep source notes readable. Use document styles for headings, body, captions and tables. Label chart units and sources; do not rely only on colour to distinguish data. Keep web layouts readable at narrow widths and controls understandable. Choose final dimensions and export formats for the actual destination.

## Delivery and review
Retain editable text, identity, background, imagery, motifs, information and notices as separate components. Deliver the editable master and requested exports with the relevant source and revision record. Check hierarchy, spelling, claims, translations, contrast, crops, font shaping, transparent edges and small text. Generated work stays a draft until reviewed. Production does not itself authorize publication.
